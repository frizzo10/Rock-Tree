How the game is played See all 1,430 cards

In a quick game you make the calls live: steals, bunts, walks, your defense and your bullpen. In a league you set most of them ahead of time. The game handles the rest, such as extra bases, tag-ups and double plays. See the calls or which ones the game makes.

Open your manager's notebook to plan your picks before the clock starts: three choices for every spot, who the computer is likely to take, and your budget. Your plan follows you from game to game.

Cards from the vault

Every player-season in the game becomes a card: his real stats, what he's worth in the game, and the roll card the dice read. These are legends. What's a legend? See the greatest seasons ever played

How to read a roll card. At every at-bat two 20-sided dice are rolled, one for the batter and one for the pitcher. The higher die decides whose card is used, then a 1-to-100 roll picks the result on that card. The wider a range, the more often it happens: Ruth's 1920 card gives him 16 slots out of 100 for a home run.

What's a legend?

A legend is one of the 45 highest-rated single seasons in the game: Babe Ruth in 1920, Barry Bonds in 2001, Pedro Martinez in 2000. Legends cost $11M to $18M under the salary cap, and each has a card the dice read like any other player's.

How you earn them

  1. Draft a team in a mock draft. Until you earn the legends, they are off the board for you and for the computer.
  2. On your report card, press Play the computer's team and play it out.
  3. Win 3 games with teams you drafted. Then all 45 legends are on the board in every mock draft after that.

Quick games with classic clubs don't count toward this. In a league or at a free table there is nothing to earn: every legend is on the board for every manager from the first day, marked with a Legend badge.
